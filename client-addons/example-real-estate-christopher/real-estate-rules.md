# Real estate rules

Hard-won rules from running real deals. These are not opinions about best
practice, they are things that cost somebody money or leverage before they
got written down.

Claude reads this when working on comps, offers, correspondence, and
property records. Edit it freely. If a rule here does not match how you
work, change it or delete it, and tell Claude what you changed.

---

## Comps

**Twelve months maximum, three to six is the target.** If there are fewer
than three sales in the window, say the set is thin. Do not reach further
back to fill it out. A stale comp is worse than a missing one because it
looks like evidence.

**Pull tight, not broad.** The tightest accurate set beats a bulk export
every time. If you need hundreds of records, that is its own named job with
a stated reason, not a comp pull.

**Location realm first, then adjust.** Select on location. HOA, pool, and
feature differences are adjustments, never exclusion filters. Excluding on
features is how you end up with four comps that agree with each other and
nothing else.

**Closed MLS, arms-length only** for anything an agent will see. No
off-market, short sales, REOs, foreclosures, family transfers, or
intercompany deals in an external comp set.

**No AVMs, and cite the source.** An estimate is not a sale.

**Never characterize a comp's condition without photos** or an in-person
look. Listing copy is marketing, not observation. "Unimproved," "standard,"
"dated" are claims, and if you cannot see it you do not know it.

**Missing permits are not a red flag on a cosmetic remodel.** Permits
trigger on structural, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, roof, and pool work. Paint
and flooring do not pull a permit anywhere.

**Verify market facts live at decision time.** Vendor pricing, what a site
still carries, whether a tool is maintained. That kind of fact rots in days,
and a note from three weeks ago will state it with total confidence.

## Offers and counters

**Never number your counters in correspondence.** "Counter Offer #2" in the
body of an email tells the other side there will be a #3. The legal form
name is fine, since that is a document type rather than a sequence.

**Every counter and offer gets a log entry, whatever the outcome.** Dead
ones too. The pattern across twenty dead offers is worth more than any
single live one.

**Soften verifiable specifics in professional correspondence.** "Strong
interest, steady showing traffic" carries the same pressure as "five
showings last week and another buyer circling," and it creates nothing
anybody can check and catch you on.

**Choose a posture on their comps before you respond.** Silent, a one-line
acknowledgment, or a full defense of your number. Default to silent. Engaging
with their comp argument makes the negotiation about their evidence.

**Stay at the draft layer.** Prepare the draft, the terms table, the
decision pack. The signature system is a separate surface with its own audit
trail, and a contract sent early cannot be unsent.

## Correspondence to appraisers

**Data only.** A Tidewater or ROV letter carries sales, adjustments, and
facts. No persuasion, no benefit framing, no adjectives about the subject.
An appraiser reading salesmanship discounts everything else in the packet.

## Property records

**One card per property, kept forever,** including after the deal dies. Next
time that address comes up you start with its history instead of from zero.

**The card is an index, not a container.** It points at the comps and the
deal file. It does not hold them.

**Default is in.** Any property that arrives with a real connection gets a
card when it arrives. Only comps and untouched bulk lists stay out. Do not
gate the card on whether the work is finished.

**Nothing on the card is hidden from anyone.** The moment you start
withholding parts of it for one audience, it stops being the index and
becomes another partial view.

**Log analysis to the card as it happens.** If you and Claude work out a
value in conversation, that goes on the card without being asked. The
conversation disappears; the card does not.

**Append only.** Date every entry and never rewrite the log. What you
believed last Tuesday is part of the record, especially when it turned out
wrong.

## People

**Contacts and properties are the lifeblood.** Everything else in the vault
serves those two.

**Relationship, not transaction, is the bar for a person file.** Showing
their listing is not a relationship. Making an offer on it is. The test is
whether you will need to know how they operate before the next call.

**Write what matters about them, not their title.** How they actually
behave, what they care about, what went sideways last time. The thing you
would want in your head thirty seconds before you dial.

## Money

**One wire per deal, never combined.** Street number and street name in the
memo, nothing else.

## Marketing copy

**Numbers are inputs, not gates.** When writing an ad or a spin, use the
numbers you have, flag anything estimated as an estimate, and render the
whole thing. Do not stop to audit the underwriting first. Those are separate
layers, and blocking one on the other means you never see either.
